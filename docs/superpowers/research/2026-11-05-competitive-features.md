# Aventa competitive feature research (2026-11-05)

Method: web search only, plus a skim of README.md and docs/superpowers/backlog.md. "Aventa lacks" means not described in those two documents; confirm against the code before scoping. Many competitor sources are marketing pages, university training PDFs or third-party blogs; these are flagged "weak" where it matters. Version-sensitive claims (Amazon Business plan tiers, Vodafone marketplace detail) could not be confirmed on first-party 2025-2026 pages.

## 1. Summary

1. Aventa's operator and seller back office (settlement, self-billing, onboarding gates, tax, roles, notifications, discount engine) is already deeper than most competitor marketing describes.
2. The biggest gaps are on the enterprise buyer side: ERP/e-procurement integration (punch-out/cXML), invoice-to-PO matching, payment terms/credit, spend analytics, reorder lists and contract-compliance guidance.
3. Telco-specific commerce gaps: device financing/trade-in, eSIM self-serve activation, number porting tracking, and a usage/IoT self-serve console.
4. Platform-level gaps: automated KYB, fraud/risk scoring, a buyer-facing AI assistant, and standards-based (TM Forum) APIs for catalogue, quote and onboarding.
5. Specs 6 (quotes) and 7 (bundles) already match what Mirakl, commercetools and Alibaba offer, so standing negotiated pricing and punch-out are the natural next step.

## 3. Recommended top 5 to build next

1. **Account price books and guided buying (row 6).** Spec 6 lets the operator answer one quote at a time; a standing per-account price list, preferred-seller flags and off-contract warnings reuse the quote data model and the agreements screen. It also closes the open backlog item where requisition unit_price is taken from the client without checking the price book. Mirakl, Amazon Business and Coupa all describe the capability, so enterprise evaluators will ask for it.
2. **Reorder lists and buyer spend analytics (rows 5 and 4, built together).** Both are small, sit entirely on data Aventa already holds (requisitions, budgets, orders), and fix the saved-basket gap in the backlog. Amazon Business leads with these in its managed-spend material. They give finance a reason to log in and give buyers repeat-purchase speed.
3. **Net terms and credit line at checkout (row 3).** The operator already runs credit & collections and billing, so this is mostly a checkout option, a limit check and an invoice due date. Amazon promotes this as a core tool for business buyers, and it is the practical alternative to card payment for enterprises. It pairs well with invoice matching later.
4. **Punch-out cXML plus invoice-to-PO matching (rows 1 and 2, as one spec).** The largest item but the one that unlocks big accounts. It needs the developer portal for credentials, requisitions as the return payload, and receipt data for matching. Build behind a feature flag with one reference procurement system; note Mirakl relies on a partner (PunchOut2Go) for punch-out, so scope carefully.
5. **Self-serve eSIM activation and number porting tracking (rows 10 and 11).** These are the telecom-specific journeys a generic marketplace cannot copy. Aventa already has the eSIM order pipeline and numbers & SIMs, so the work is the customer-visible QR/reissue and a spec-6-style status timeline for port requests. Check what the eSIM screens already show before sizing.

Not in the top 5 despite value: device financing and trade-in (L, needs credit decisioning and legal input; best after net terms proves the credit path) and automated KYB (M, but needs a vendor choice).

## 4. What Aventa already does that competitors highlight

- Quotes, demo requests and tracked enquiries (spec 6): Mirakl lists quote request management and customer-specific pricing (https://mirakl.com/use-cases/drop-ship); commercetools models quotes and quote requests (https://docs.commercetools.com/api/associates-overview); TM Forum defines a quote API (https://projects.tmforum.org/wiki/x/C164C).
- Approval chains with budgets and roles: commercetools Approval Rules/Flows and Associate Roles (https://docs.commercetools.com/api/associates-overview); Amazon Business approval workflows (https://business.amazon.in/en/discover-more/blog/purchasing-approval-workflows).
- Seller onboarding with gates and evidence: Mirakl and AppDirect market partner onboarding (https://appdirect.com/platform/marketplace-platform; https://mirakl.com/use-cases/drop-ship). Aventa's seven gates with dual control and gate policy appear to go further than these pages describe; this compares marketing text with the README, not products.
- Seller settlement, self-billing and payouts: Mirakl Payout and AppDirect automated multi-party reconciliation (https://www.mirakl.com/products/payout/; https://appdirect.com/platform/marketplace-platform).
- Multi-currency and tax configuration: AppDirect lists multi-currency and merchant-of-record handling (https://appdirect.com/platform/marketplace-platform).
- Subscriptions and renewals: AppDirect subscription lifecycle (https://appdirect.com/platform/marketplace-platform).
- Buyer-built configurable bundles (spec 7, in progress): none of the searches surfaced an equivalent bundle builder at the competitors named, so this may be a differentiator. Not confirmed.
- Developer portal and partner integrations (endpoints, retries, events): in line with Mirakl's self-service onboarding with API, EDI and connectors (https://mirakl.com/use-cases/drop-ship).
- Operator-run, multi-market marketplaces: Vodafone Business runs 13 marketplaces on AppDirect (https://businesswire.com/news/home/20230808302478/en/Vodafone-Business-Selects-AppDirect-as-its-Partner-to-Accelerate-Success-in-the-Channel). Aventa's "Define a marketplace" with per-market policy is the same shape.
