Journeys you can drive to completion

These are not click-throughs. Every action below changes the underlying records, and the change shows up wherever it should — including in the other three portals, which share one dataset.

  • Partner onboarding, seven gates. Open partner.html, Onboarding. Resolve the failing webhook, then the sandbox order — the technical gate only closes when both are done, at which point compliance review opens and the security questionnaire unlocks by itself. Answer it, then publish. The partner goes live, gains the Security Marketplace, and it becomes selectable in the listing wizard.
  • Product onboarding. partner.html, New listing. Six steps; the commission split recalculates as you type a price. Submitting creates a real product and puts it in the operator's review queue — open operator.html, Catalogue and it is there. Submitting without a name or price is refused and sends you back to the step that is missing.
  • Catalogue governance. operator.html, Catalogue. Approving publishes the listing live; rejecting sends it back with your reason. The listing that breaches content policy 7.4 has Approve disabled — you can only reject it or ask the partner.
  • Consumer purchase. consumer.html. Add a handset and a streaming subscription, check out. Two orders are created on their correct pipelines — shipped for the handset, instant for the subscription — and the subscription starts and appears under Subscriptions. Cancel it and access runs to the paid-to date; pause it and billing stops; restart it and it comes back.
  • Enterprise requisition. enterprise.html. A basket becomes a requisition, not an order. Add MDR seats and it needs IT sign-off; add a €2,450 bundle and it needs finance. Approve in Approvals and the order is placed, seats are added to the existing subscription, and budget consumed moves. Decline it and nothing is ordered. Something under the threshold with no security in it orders straight away.
  • Operator onboarding queue. operator.html, Partner onboarding. Clear Northwind Mobility's gates one at a time; on the last one the partner becomes live and joins the partner directory.
  • Settlement run. operator.html, Settlement runs. Approving requires typing APPROVE. Statements move to approved — and stay unpaid until the cycle date, because approving is not paying.
  • My details is fully working. consumer.html, My details. Add a card and the number, expiry and security code are validated and only the last four digits are kept. Exactly one payment method and one address is always the default, and the last working one cannot be removed. Top up the wallet, redeem points into credit, add and edit addresses with a PIN check, request your data, or schedule account closure 30 days out and stop it again from the banner.
  • Storefront tools are real. Coverage returns a survey for known PIN codes and says Not surveyed where a network has not been measured. Trade-in quotes by model and condition and the credit comes off the basket. The bundle builder gives 5% per subscription capped at 15%, refuses a fourth, and drops the real items in your basket.
  • Users, in all four portals. A directory showing role, MFA, password age and strength. Inviting refuses a bad email, the new person lands as Invited with no permissions in effect, changing a role takes effect at their next sign-in, and removing someone needs REMOVE typed. You cannot remove yourself. On the consumer account the same screen carries household members and monthly spend caps.
  • Roles configuration, in all four portals. The capability matrix is editable — every cell cycles none, scoped, full. Create a role from nothing or clone one, and it becomes a column straight away with nobody in it. Built-in roles cannot be deleted, and neither can a custom role someone still holds — the dialog lists who, and sends you to reassign them.
  • Passwords, MFA and sessions. Change your own password and anything below policy is refused, a mismatch is refused, strength is scored live and other sessions are signed out. On someone else's account, send a reset link or force a reset — force needs RESET typed, flags the account and clears their sessions. Turning MFA off needs OFF typed.
  • The invoice, before the money moves. operator.html, Settlement runs. Open any statement to read the self-billing invoice — reference, period, every SKU that sold, the deduction stack from gross to net, and the tax line. The order lines reconcile to the statement gross to the cent. Approve sits inside the document; a statement with an open dispute shows it disabled with the reason.
  • The avatar opens a real account menu. My details, change password, sign-in and security, notification preferences and what your role can do — in all four portals. My details is a working screen: saving refuses an empty name or a bad email, writes to the user record and updates the top bar. Mark yourself away and it tells you plainly that work will wait until you name a delegate.
  • Define a marketplace. operator.html, Marketplaces. It needs a name and a line of storefront copy, starts closed to buyers, copies the policy you base it on, and says it has no partners rather than showing an encouraging zero. Closing a live one needs CLOSE typed and states what happens to listings, recurring orders and sellers.
  • Catalogue policy. Ten rules per marketplace, each enforce / warn / off. Enforce blocks publication; warn only appears on the checklist. The listing review inspector links to the policy behind the check it just failed.
  • Ask the partner. operator.html, Catalogue, review a listing. It names the recipient, refuses a query with no ask in it, records a real query with a working-day due date, and can hold the listing so the delay lands on the seller rather than on your desk.
  • Gate policy. operator.html, Partner onboarding. Owner, target days and dual control per gate. KYC and Agreements cannot be made waivable, and the toggles are disabled rather than quietly ignored.
  • Tax configuration. operator.html, Tax. Six jurisdictions, each with its own rate and — the fact everything follows from — whether the marketplace or the seller is merchant of record. A jurisdiction cannot go active without a registration number. Partner withholding follows the tax certificate; recording one releases it. The buyer states its GSTIN and sees whether input credit is claimable; the consumer storefront reads tax-inclusive pricing from the same configuration.
  • Export writes a file. Every Export names its columns, offers on-screen versus everything and CSV versus JSON, then generates and downloads it. Values come from the record, not the HTML, so a status exports as its code rather than as a chip's label. Chart screens export the figures behind the chart.
  • Product media. partner.html, New listing. Several images plus a video and a datasheet, a primary image that drives the card, reordering, and alt text that is required rather than optional — the listing will not submit without it.
  • Fulfilment APIs. partner.html, Integrations. Register the endpoints the marketplace calls, with auth, retry and timeout. Plain HTTP and unauthenticated production endpoints are refused. Send a test call reflects the endpoint's real state — the broken one fails. The call log answers "we never received it".
  • Cost, list and sale price. The wizard shows the full margin stack and refuses a listing priced at or below cost. The seller chooses how far the marketplace may discount, and the screen states the lowest a promotion could reach.
  • Bundle discounts, floored at cost. operator.html, Catalogue. Discount each component individually, capped at that component's own cost. An override below the cost of the parts is raised to cost on save.
  • The discount engine. operator.html, Promotions. Time of day, day of week, cart value, item count, category, buyer type, first order. The screen says what applies and what does not, with the reason. No promotion may sell below cost — that is a floor in the engine, not a rule you can write around. Move the clock and watch a time-of-day rule fire.
  • The reporting period switches for real. The 90-day and 12-month tabs sit on a trailing 12-month series whose last three months sum exactly to the live order set, so the two views reconcile. Totals, growth and the monthly chart all change. Over 12 months there is no comparable prior window, so it says so rather than inventing a growth figure.
  • The partner's own application is readable. partner.html, Onboarding. Each of the seven gates opens what was submitted — why they applied, the ownership carried over, the reused settlement account, and at the open gate the webhook URL with its HTTP 500 failure stated. Guide is a real document covering every gate, its owner, its target and its evidence.
  • The seller can download its bills. partner.html, Settlement. Every statement, outstanding and paid, openable as the invoice and downloadable as a CSV of order lines plus the gross-to-net stack.
  • Notification rules and message content. New rule is a working builder — event, audience, channels, severity, throttle — and it refuses a rule with no channel because that rule would do nothing. Every rule then has editable wording with a tab per channel: merge tokens are click-to-insert, the preview shows sample values rather than braces, SMS counts characters and segments, and switching channel keeps what you typed.
  • Onboarding gates open their inputs. operator.html, Partner onboarding. Each of the seven gates is a button. Open one to read exactly what was submitted — beneficial ownership at KYC, the settlement account and treaty certificate at Bank & tax, the webhook and sandbox order at Technical readiness — with who submitted it, who reviewed it, and the evidence checklist ticked or not. A gate not yet reached says so instead of inventing a record.
  • Commercial models. operator.html, Commission plans. A plan is a model plus its parameters, and switching model swaps the parameters, relabels the rate and hides the tier table for a flat-rate model. Define a new model with its own parameter list and it becomes selectable in the wizard without leaving it.
  • Partner bills. operator.html, Partners. Every statement for a partner — outstanding and paid — openable as the self-billing invoice and downloadable. A bill download is the order lines plus the gross-to-net deduction stack, not a picture of a total.
  • First-party products and bundles. operator.html, Catalogue. Pull components from the operator's own BSS catalogue and the price derives itself — 4% off per extra component, capped at 18%. An override above the parts is called out. What is created is first party: no partner, no commission, live immediately.
  • Billing configuration. operator.html, Billing configuration. Edit the numbering pattern and watch it flow into the live preview and into real statement references. Then change a partner's bill cycle — it will not save without a stated reason, because an override with no reason is how a cycle quietly drifts. Three partners here already differ from their plan.
  • Notification rules, in all four portals. Rules address a role, not a person — so moving someone between roles changes what they are told without editing a rule. Toggles are live, SMS is reserved for urgent severity, and the delivery log shows a bounced address being retired.

Dismissing any dialog changes nothing, and high-risk actions stay disabled until the confirmation word is typed. 266 automated journey checks assert all of this — see the README.
